What drawings does a restaurant build-out need for a permit?

Requirements vary by city and county, so always confirm with your local building department. But most restaurant build-outs are reviewed against the same set of drawings.

The drawings most cities ask for

  • 1. Cover sheet and code summary. The project information and the code summary: occupancy type, occupant load, construction type and the codes the design follows. It's often the first sheet a reviewer reads.
  • 2. Floor plan and layout. The dining area, kitchen, restrooms, exits and equipment positions, drawn to scale. Reviewers use it to check occupancy, exits and clearances, and accessible restrooms, routes and counters.
  • 3. Equipment and finish schedule. The kitchen equipment list and the wall, floor and ceiling finishes, plus the required sinks: hand sinks, a three-compartment sink and a mop sink. Health departments usually ask for it.
  • 4. Mechanical (HVAC and kitchen exhaust). Heating and cooling, ventilation, and the kitchen hood with its exhaust and make-up air. The hood is often the item that draws the most review comments.
  • 5. Plumbing. Fixtures, floor drains, water heating and, where required, a grease interceptor. Health departments often review this alongside the building department.
  • 6. Electrical. Panel and load calculations, lighting, receptacles, and the circuits for kitchen equipment.
  • 7. Energy code compliance. Forms showing the lighting and HVAC meet the local energy code. Many jurisdictions ask for them.
  • 8. Fire protection. The hood fire-suppression system and, where required, sprinklers and alarm. The hood suppression system is often submitted separately by the suppression contractor.
  • 9. Structural, when the work needs it. Needed when you cut into walls, add openings or support heavy rooftop equipment.

Why plans get sent back

A common reason is that the trades don't agree with each other, for example a hood that doesn't match the exhaust drawn on the mechanical sheet. Drawing every trade together before you submit avoids a second review round.

Questions to ask your building department before you start

  • Which drawings do you require for my building type?
  • Is the health-department review a separate submission?
  • Do the drawings need a licensed professional's stamp?
